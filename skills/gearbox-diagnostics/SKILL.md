# Gearbox diagnostics

## Common failure modes

| Mode | What tends to show it |
| --- | --- |
| Lubrication breakdown | Oil temperature rising faster than load explains; oil debris or filter alarms |
| Bearing wear | Bearing temperature rising against oil temperature; vibration at bearing frequencies |
| Gear tooth damage | Vibration at gear mesh frequencies and their sidebands; debris |
| Oil cooling failure | Oil temperature rising with ambient temperature and load; cooler or fan alarms |
| Sensor fault | One reading moving alone, implausibly fast, or marked suspect |

## Telling them apart

- Compare oil and bearing temperature: a bearing running hotter than the oil points at the bearing.
- A temperature rise that tracks power is load; one that persists at low power is not.
- Check the turbine's earlier cases on the gearbox: a repeat after an oil change suggests the cause was not the oil.
- Always consider a sensor fault before a mechanical one when only one reading moves.

## Citing

- Take limits and inspection intervals only from the approved manuals, cited by passage. Never quote a limit from
  memory.
