## 1. Scope

How technicians reach and leave the turbines at Dogger East and Hornsea West. It applies with the manufacturer's
safety procedure, WS-SAF-001; where the two differ, the stricter governs.

## 2. Transfer limits

Transfer from the crew transfer vessel to the turbine's boat landing goes ahead only within all of these, over the
transfer and the planned return.

| Condition | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Significant wave height | 1.5 m |
| Mean wind speed at 10 m | 15 m/s |
| Visibility | 1,000 m |
| Daylight | Transfers start no earlier than 30 minutes after sunrise, and are back aboard 30 minutes before sunset |

The vessel's master may refuse a transfer at any time.

## 3. Marine coordination

1. Every transfer is logged with the marine coordination centre: who, which turbine, when.
2. Each technician carries a personal locator beacon and wears a lifejacket during transfer.
3. A turbine left unattended for a planned return has its boat landing checked by the vessel before collection.

## 4. Stranding

If the weather closes in while technicians are on a turbine, they stay in the turbine's refuge, with its survival
kit, until the vessel or a helicopter can collect them. Plan every visit with the refuge's supplies checked.

## 5. Costs to plan for

A crew transfer vessel day is the largest cost of most offshore repairs. Group work on nearby turbines into one
vessel day where the risk allows.
