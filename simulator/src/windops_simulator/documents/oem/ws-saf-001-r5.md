## 1. Scope

This procedure covers climbing the tower, working in the nacelle and the hub, and working on the blades from the
nacelle, on the WS-8.0-167 and the WS-4.5-145. The site's own access procedure applies as well, and where the two
differ the stricter one governs. Nothing in a recommendation, a plan or a work order replaces the judgement of the
person in charge of the work on the day.

## 2. Before the work

1. A work permit is issued for the turbine, the task and the window, by a person authorised to do so.
2. At least two people trained in work at height and rescue go together. Nobody climbs or works alone.
3. A rescue plan for the turbine is in place, and its rescue kit is in the nacelle and checked.
4. The weather is checked for the whole planned window, from a forecast retrieved no more than 3 hours before the
   decision (section 3).
5. The turbine is stopped from the controller, and isolated and locked out before any work on rotating or energised
   equipment. Each person fits their own lock.

## 3. Weather limits

Climbing and work at height stop, or do not start, when the forecast or the conditions on site exceed any limit
below over the planned window.

| Condition | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Mean wind speed at hub height | 12 m/s |
| Gusts | 18 m/s |
| Thunderstorm or lightning | Any within 10 km, or a forecast of thunderstorms over the window |
| Visibility, offshore | As the site's access procedure sets |

- A forecast older than 3 hours is stale. Do not decide on it: retrieve a new one.
- When no forecast can be had, treat the limits as exceeded. Missing weather is never a reason to proceed.
- The person in charge on site may stop work at any time, on what they see, whatever the forecast says.
- On a thunderstorm warning, leave the turbine, or if that is not possible, shelter in the tower's base, away from
  the tower wall and the ladder.

## 4. In the hub

1. Pitch all blades to feather.
2. Apply the rotor lock, and confirm it is engaged before entering the hub.
3. Never enter the hub with the rotor turning, or with the rotor lock not engaged.

## 5. Stopping work

Work stops at once when a limit is exceeded, a person feels unwell, equipment is damaged, or anyone involved
believes it is unsafe. Restart needs the person in charge's agreement.

## 6. What changed in revision 5

The forecast's age limit, 3 hours, is new. Section 3 now says that missing weather is treated as exceeding the
limits.
